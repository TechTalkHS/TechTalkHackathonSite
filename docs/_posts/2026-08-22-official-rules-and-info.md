**TechTalk HS Hackathon 2026**

*Hack for Good*

Official Rules & Participant Guide

# **1\. Overview**

TechTalk HS Hackathon 2026: Hack for Good is a two-day hackathon where high school students build coded solutions to real problems. Every project must solve a genuine issue — no purely abstract or novelty builds — and must be solved through code, not just a pitch or design concept. The event runs over two separate days (participants go home overnight); it is not an overnight stay.

***One-line pitch: Build something that actually helps.***

# **2\. Eligibility**

* Open to all currently enrolled high school students (grades 9–12).

* **PLEASE BRING YOUR LAPTOP**

* No prior coding or hackathon experience required — all skill levels welcome.

* Participants under 18 must have a signed parent/guardian consent & liability waiver on file before the event.

* Participants are expected to attend both days — teams pick up exactly where they left off, so partial attendance affects the team's build time.

* Estimated turnout: 20–30 participants.

# **3\. Registration**

* Register individually or as a pre-formed team of 2–3 (max team size: 4).

* Registration deadline: \[Date\].

* Solo registrants will be placed into a team by organizers before the event.

* A signed waiver/consent form is required to check in on Day 1\.

# **4\. Sector Tracks & Team Structure**

Every participant is assigned to one of five career-sector groups, where teams build coded solutions related to that field. Teams stay in the same sector for both days — no reshuffling between build blocks — to preserve momentum and clear project ownership:

* Healthcare & Wellness

* Environmental & Sustainability

* Business & Finance

* Education

* Public Safety & Community

With an expected 20–30 participants across 5 sectors, teams are sized at 2–3 people (4 max), putting roughly 2 teams per sector.

# **5\. Guest Speaker Format**

Five guest speakers total — one per sector, each a working professional in that field. Every speaker addresses all participants together as one group, not just their own sector, so every student gets exposure to how technology applies across every field, not only the one they're building in. After each talk, participants return to their assigned sector groups to continue building.

# **7\. Project Requirements**

* Each team selects one prompt from their sector's prompt list (below) at the start of Build Block 1 on Day 1 — the prompt must be solved through code, not a poster, slide deck, or pitch alone.

* Projects must be started and substantially built during the event — light pre-planning (idea, wireframes) is allowed, but the actual coding happens during event build blocks.

* No work on the project is allowed between Day 1 and Day 2 — teams pick up exactly where they left off.

* Any codeable format is allowed: web app, mobile app, chatbot, script, data tool, or similar — as long as it is functional code, not just a design mockup.

* Each team submits: a short project description, working code with a demo, and a short presentation.

# **8\. Sector Prompts**

Each sector has a curated list of real-world issues. Teams choose one prompt from their sector to solve by building actual code — these are starting points, and teams may narrow or adapt the problem as long as it stays within the spirit of the prompt.

**Healthcare & Wellness**

* A symptom-checker or triage chatbot that helps someone figure out next steps before seeing a doctor.

* A tool to help students track mood/stress over time and surface relevant resources.

* A medication or appointment reminder app for patients who forget doses or visits.

* An accessibility tool that helps elderly or disabled users book healthcare appointments online.

**Environmental & Sustainability**

* An app that tracks and helps reduce household or school energy/water usage.

* A tool to map and report local environmental issues (illegal dumping, pollution hotspots).

* A carbon-footprint calculator based on daily commute or food choices.

* A food-waste tracking tool for school cafeterias or households.

**Business & Finance**

* A budgeting app built for teens or first-time earners.

* A tool that helps small local businesses manage inventory or orders.

* A platform matching students with local job or gig opportunities.

* An interactive financial-literacy simulation or quiz game.

**Education**

* A personalized study scheduler that adapts to a student's workload and deadlines.

* A peer-tutoring matching platform connecting students who need help with those who can give it.

* An accessibility tool for students with learning differences (e.g., text-to-speech reader).

* A homework-help chatbot trained on a specific class's material.

**Public Safety & Community**

* A neighborhood issue-reporting app (potholes, broken streetlights, unsafe areas).

* An emergency alert or contact system for schools.

* A platform connecting volunteers with community needs (food banks, shelters, cleanups).

* A disaster-preparedness checklist and notification tool for families.

# **9\. Presentations & Audience Vote**

There are no outside judges — winners are decided by the participants themselves.

* Every team presents their project to the entire audience of participants (not just their own sector).

* Each presentation is brief — enough to show the problem, the demo, and why it matters.

* After all presentations, every participant casts a vote for their favorite project (excluding their own team's).

* Votes are tallied live or immediately after presentations conclude, and the top project(s) are announced during Awards & Closing.

# **10\. Code of Conduct**

* Treat all participants, mentors, speakers, and organizers with respect.

* No harassment, discrimination, or disruptive behavior — violations may result in removal from the event.

* Work must be original to the event; plagiarized or pre-built projects will be disqualified.

* Follow all venue rules, including any restrictions on food, equipment, and building access.

# **11\. Safety, Waivers & Liability**

* All participants must have a signed parent/guardian waiver on file prior to Day 1\.

* Event organizers will maintain event liability insurance covering both event days, as required by the venue.

* Participants are responsible for their own personal belongings and devices between days.

* In case of emergency, follow posted venue emergency procedures and notify an organizer immediately.
